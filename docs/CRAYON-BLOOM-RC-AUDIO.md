# CRAYON BLOOM RC audio

The score and every sound are generated by `src/bloom-audio.js` using Web Audio. There are no downloaded samples, recordings, third-party melodies, or external audio dependencies. The hand-authored motifs and arrangements were made for this project; no existing song was used as a reference. The file is copied to `dist/bloom-audio.js` for deployment.

## Sound design

- Home: 86 BPM, original eight-motif mallet melody, quiet plucked lower accompaniment, sparse paper percussion.
- Game: 101 BPM arrangement with slightly more pulse and occasional half-beat notes; the same warm instrument palette.
- Both arrangements use a 128-beat cycle (about 89 seconds at home / 76 seconds in game), with four phrase arrangements rather than an obvious 30-second recording loop.
- Rotate: short filtered paper noise and a quiet high tone.
- Hold: soft paper swish and pluck.
- Drop: brief descending wooden tock, immediate start.
- Clear: crayon-like scratch and three warm ascending notes.
- Combo: bounded rising motif, maximum pitch boost at combo six.
- Good item / tutorial success: bright three-note phrase.
- Bad item: small descending pluck and paper sound, without ominous sounds.
- Game over: four quiet falling notes.
- Ordinary lateral movement and repeat cells deliberately have no sound.

## Runtime contract

`window.BloomAudio` exposes `configure(saved)`, `unlock()`, `scene(name)`, `effect(type, power)`, `clear()`, `destroy()`, and read-only `stats()`.

The existing `saved.sound` remains the master switch. `saved.bgm !== false` and `saved.sfx !== false` opt into their respective buses when the master is enabled. Missing new settings preserve the old master choice. This module never writes localStorage.

The caller invokes `unlock()` within an existing user interaction. It creates at most one live AudioContext, returns synchronously, and catches rejected resumes. Game start never awaits audio. Scene values are `home`, `game`, `paused`, `over`, and `off`; game over permits its one-shot effect while disabling music. Pause, off, and document visibility immediately cancel music scheduling, stop voices, and suspend the context.

One 80ms look-ahead timeout schedules at most two beats per callback. A delayed callback skips elapsed time instead of emitting missed notes. AudioContext time schedules the voices independently of gameplay frame time. A maximum of 28 live voices, including a 16-voice music limit, bounds rapid effects. Ended nodes disconnect; noise reuses one 0.4-second buffer. Only three persistent gain nodes remain between games.

## Validation and limits

`node --test tests/bloom-audio.test.cjs` covers lazy startup, repeated config/unlock, original score scheduling, independent settings, visibility and lifecycle cleanup, all effects, rapid-call limits, stalled callbacks, unsupported browsers, rejected resumes, and matching source/build artifacts.

The 30-minute scheduler test uses **virtual time**, not a real 30-minute browser/phone play session. It checks bounded timer/node counts, not physical-device CPU cost or perceptual audio quality. Android speakers/headphones still require user review for BGM volume, SFX balance, warmth, and seamless listening. Mobile autoplay resume also needs real-device validation.
